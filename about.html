OUR STORY

Built on one fire, one cove, and a shared spice bench

We're a small, independent restaurant — not a chain, and not backed by any outside brand. Here's the honest version of how we got started and what we're still working on.

WHERE IT STARTED

A single charcoal pit at the edge of the water

Spice Grill Cove started small: one grill, a handful of tables, and a habit of buying whatever the boats brought in that morning instead of ordering from a set list. The spice bench came together the same way — jars accumulated one trip at a time, and the blends we still use were worked out through a lot of ordinary trial and error, not a single big idea.

Over time the fire got bigger and the dining room grew around it, but the basic approach never changed: cook what's fresh, season it with care, and let the grill do most of the talking.

HOW WE COOK

A few things we hold to, meal after meal

These aren't marketing lines — they're the practical habits that shape every plate that leaves the kitchen.

Buy the day's catch first

We plan the seafood side of the menu around what's actually available that morning, rather than locking in a fixed list months in advance.

Mix spice blends in-house

Our rubs and marinades are ground and mixed on-site, in small batches, rather than bought pre-made — it's slower, but it lets us adjust for the ingredient in front of us.

Cook over real fire

Everything that can go over hardwood charcoal does. It takes longer than a flat-top and it's less forgiving, but it's the flavor the whole menu is built around.

A ROUGH TIMELINE

How the restaurant has grown

Early days

One grill, a short menu

We opened with a handful of dishes built around whatever spice blends we'd settled on that week, cooked entirely over charcoal.

Growing

Building relationships with local suppliers

As the kitchen grew, so did our relationships with the people supplying our fish, produce, and meat — which is what lets the "catch of the day" approach actually work.

Today

A full dining room, still cooking the same way

The dining room and patio have grown, but the kitchen still runs on the same habits: real fire, fresh ingredients, and spice blends made in-house.
